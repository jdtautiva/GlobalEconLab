# GlobalEcon Lab — Interactive Study Guides

> **Interactive self-study resources for undergraduate International Economics courses.**
> Developed by **Julián Díaz Tautiva, PhD**

---

## Overview

****Economía Internacional** — Interactive Study Guides** is a collection of nine browser-based pedagogical resources designed to support autonomous learning, review, and consolidation of core concepts in International Economics.

Each guide is organized as a self-contained learning module that combines concise conceptual explanations, key economic relationships, formulas, graphical representations, worked examples, short activities, integrative cases, and interactive self-assessment.

The collection progresses from the historical foundations of international economic thought to classical and neoclassical trade theory, economies of scale, intra-industry trade, trade policy, and regional economic integration.

Each resource runs as a standalone HTML file. No backend, installation, or external JavaScript libraries are required for the core learning functionality.

---

## Learning Objectives

By working through the complete collection, students are able to:

* Explain the transition from mercantilist thinking to Adam Smith's conception of specialization, markets, and national wealth
* Interpret the classical quantity theory of money and the international adjustment mechanism associated with monetary flows
* Distinguish absolute advantage from comparative advantage and derive trade patterns using opportunity costs and the Ricardian model
* Analyze short-run trade and income-distribution effects using the specific-factors model
* Apply the Heckscher–Ohlin framework and interpret the Stolper–Samuelson, Rybczynski, Heckscher–Ohlin, and factor-price-equalization results
* Explain how internal and external economies of scale can generate specialization, industrial concentration, agglomeration, and path dependence
* Distinguish inter-industry from intra-industry trade and calculate measures such as the **Grubel–Lloyd** and **Brülhart** indices
* Evaluate tariffs, subsidies, quotas, effective protection, and other trade-policy instruments
* Compare free trade agreements with deeper processes of regional economic integration
* Connect theoretical predictions with numerical exercises, applied cases, policy decisions, and distributional consequences

## Key Features

**9 standalone learning modules** — each topic is delivered as an independent HTML resource, allowing instructors to assign individual guides according to the sequence of the course or combine them as a complete review package.

**Structured autonomous-learning pathway** — every guide begins with a set of *ideas fuerza* that summarize the central propositions of the topic, followed by a visual conceptual route that organizes the economic reasoning students should follow.

**Progressive conceptual development** — material moves from definitions and assumptions to mechanisms, mathematical relationships, graphical interpretation, applications, and integration with previously studied models.

**Economic formulas and visual representations** — the resources incorporate formulas, comparative frameworks, conceptual chains, tables, and inline SVG economic diagrams to connect verbal reasoning with formal economic analysis.

**Short applied activities** — expandable activities throughout the modules ask students to classify concepts, reconstruct economic mechanisms, calculate indicators, identify winners and losers, analyze changes in equilibrium, or interpret policy scenarios.

**Integrative cases** — modules use applied economies, countries, regions, or policy situations to move students from isolated theoretical concepts toward complete economic diagnosis.

**Interactive self-assessment** — the nine guides contain **74 multiple-choice self-assessment questions** in total. Responses are evaluated immediately, with the correct alternative identified and explanatory feedback provided after each answer.

**Resettable quizzes** — students can restart each self-assessment and attempt the complete set of questions again, making the resources suitable for repeated practice before examinations or classroom discussion.

**Mastery checklist** — each module ends with a *Lista de dominio* that helps students verify whether they can explain, calculate, distinguish, and apply the principal concepts before moving to the next topic.

**Quick navigation** — a persistent navigation bar provides direct access to the major sections of each resource, including key ideas, conceptual models, applications, cases, and self-assessment.

**Print and PDF support** — every guide includes a dedicated **Print or Save as PDF** option. Print-specific CSS removes interactive controls and reformats the content for study, distribution, or offline review.

**Responsive and accessibility-oriented interface** — layouts adapt to smaller screens, while semantic labels, visible keyboard focus, accessible descriptions, and screen-reader support are incorporated into the interface.

## Suggested Use

The resources can be used independently or as a sequenced learning pathway. They are particularly suited for:

* Pre-class preparation
* Post-lecture reinforcement
* Examination review
* Guided autonomous study
* In-class conceptual discussion
* Numerical and graphical practice
* Case-based application
* Formative self-assessment

Because each guide is independent, instructors can integrate selected modules into an LMS, distribute the HTML files directly to students, host them on a web server, or provide printable/PDF versions.
